#### **First impression**

I landed in the morning. After almost a full day of traveling, my brain was working roughly like an old laptop with 2% battery left. The first thing that really impressed me wasn't the skyscrapers or the neon. It was how quiet such a huge city could be. You can be standing on a tiny street ten minutes away from a major station — and that's it. A bicycle, a vending machine, a couple of elderly people, the smell of food. Then you turn a corner and suddenly you're completely swallowed by a stream of people. Tokyo is all about contrasts.

#### **Shibuya**

First thing I did was go to Shibuya Crossing. Honestly? It looks bigger on the internet. But when you're actually standing there, there's a different feeling: you're just a tiny part of a huge machine that works perfectly well without you. After crossing, I went into the nearest konbini. And that's where the real Japan began. I bought an onigiri, a cold green tea, and some completely mysterious sweet with a cat on the packaging. The sweet was awful. The onigiri, on the other hand, was incredible. 

In the evening I went for a walk without a route. This turned out to be one of the best decisions of the entire trip. My rule was: If the street looks more interesting than the one I'm currently walking on — turn. Sometimes that led to beautiful places. Sometimes it led to a parking lot. But it was never boring.
